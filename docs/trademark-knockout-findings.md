**Status: screened 2026-09-19 against `main` at `2d6fbc0`. Nothing was filed,

Camber on the register, Aimless and Detourist on common-law use. Longcut

Answers [`trademark-knockout-brief.md`](trademark-knockout-brief.md). Corrects

## This is a knockout search, not a clearance search

**Nobody here is a lawyer and nothing below is legal advice.**

A knockout screen looks for obvious blockers in public sources and kills the
candidates that have one. It is cheap, it is fast, and **it cannot establish that
a name is safe to use.** A clearance search is a different product: it covers
state registers, phonetic and foreign-equivalent variants, unregistered
common-law users found through commercial databases, and it ends in a written
opinion from someone whose professional liability stands behind it.

The three verdicts used below are the only three this document is entitled to
reach:

- **no knockout blocker found — proceed to professional clearance**
- **blocked, drop it**
- **cannot be determined from public sources**

The words *clear* and *available* do not appear as verdicts anywhere in this
document, and if you find yourself about to write them in a document that cites
this one, that is the mistake this section exists to prevent.

## 1. The positive control, reported first

The method was run on **"Scenic"** before anything else, because the answer is
known: there is a senior, established, directly-competing navigation app of
exactly that name. If the method cannot find it, nothing else the method says is
worth reading.

**The method has two legs, and they did not agree.**

| Leg | What it returned for "Scenic" | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Federal register** | 400 marks containing the word; 23 in class 9, of which 5 live. **None of them is the competitor.** | ❌ **missed it** |
| **App Store / web** | *Scenic — Motorcycle Navigation*, Applified Life Ltd., bundle `com.pinguido.scenic`, released 2016-07-21, primary category **Navigation**, `scenic.app` | ✅ found it, first result |

The competitor **holds no US federal trademark record at all.** An owner search
for "Applified" returns two unrelated entities (Applified BV, Netherlands, and
Applified Inc, Delaware), both dead; "Pinguido" returns nothing. A company with
~30M hours ridden and nine years of priority in the identical field is invisible
to the register.

**The register leg still knocked "Scenic" out — by a different party.** `SCENIC`,
Scenic Tours Europe AG (Switzerland), serial **79431580**, live §66(a) Madrid
designation filed 2025-05-13, covering IC 009, 012, 016, 025, 035, 039, 041, 042
and 043. Classes 9 and 39 are this product's classes. That is a live blocker the
brainstorm did not know about.

**So the control passes as a whole and fails one leg, and the failure is the
finding.** The single most dangerous conflicting user of a name, in this exact
field, was invisible to the federal register — which is the concrete form of the
warning that a register hit is not the only way to be blocked. Read every
"register: nothing found" below with that in mind. It is why sections 3–6 each
carry a separate, labelled common-law block, and why two of the three knockouts
came from there.

## 4. Aimless — blocked, drop it

The block is **not** on the register. It is a shipping app.

### Common-law signal *(this is the knockout)*

**`Aimless Drives`** — Bryce Percoco, App Store ID 6799342576, bundle
`com.brycepercoco.aimless`, primary category **Navigation** (also Travel),
released **2026-09-02**, v1.0.2 on 2026-09-05. Its own description:

> "Aimless makes driving loops that start and end exactly where you are. Pick how
> long you want to be out — 30, 60, 90, or 120 minutes — and Aimless hands you
> three routes that wander out and come back without retracing themselves."

It also says it filters out loops with significant highway content, and that its
durations come from routing the exact waypoints it hands off.

**That is this project's loop mode, in the same App Store category, under the
word we were going to use as the whole brand** — and it calls itself "Aimless"
throughout, not "Aimless Drives". Its first public use is 2026-09-02, which is
earlier than any first use this project could now establish. Common-law rights
come from use, not registration.

`branding-brainstorm.md` §3 could not have caught this: it ran its App Store
check on 2026-09-01.

| `AIMLESS` | A.B.E.L. Technologies, LLC (Nebraska) | 50017512 / — | 042 | On-line non-downloadable AI software for model selection, prompt routing, contextual memory, workflow management, cost optimisation | **LIVE**, filed 2026-07-28, **§1(b) intent-to-use**, not yet published |

## 7. Two inherited claims, checked

### "Skoal Long Cut is class 34, so conflict risk is low"

`branding-brainstorm.md` §3 records this as Longcut's honest risk. **The
conclusion is right and the reasoning given for it is not the reason.**

What the register actually says:

- `SKOAL LONG CUT`, serial 75661606, reg. 2523910 — **cancelled 2022-07-15**, and
  the registration **disclaims "LONG CUT"**. The earlier `SKOAL LONG CUT`
  (reg. 1349913, 1984) is also dead.
- The same disclaimer pattern runs through the class 34 family: "long cut"
  describes a cut of tobacco, so it appears in everyone's mark — Grizzly, Kodiak,
  Husky, Marlboro — and belongs to none of them.
- `LONG CUT` alone is owned by Everhard Products (reg. 3848570) for **roofing
  knives**, with "CUT" disclaimed.

**Why the reasoning matters even though the answer holds.** Class numbers are an
administrative filing convenience, not the test. TMEP §1207.01(d)(v) — quoted
routinely in USPTO office actions — states that the classification of goods and
services has no bearing on likelihood of confusion; what controls is how the
goods are identified. "Different class, therefore low risk" would have produced a
false all-clear against Camber Places, whose blocking registration sits in class
9 *and* class 39.

The defensible version: **no one owns "LONG CUT" as a mark in tobacco** (it is
disclaimed as descriptive), the Skoal registrations are **dead**, and the goods
share no trade channel with a navigation app.

The *association* question the brainstorm raised — that some Americans hear
chewing tobacco first — is untouched by any of this. It is a branding question
and it stands. So does the brainstorm's advice to ask five people.

### `longcut.app` — settled

`branding-brainstorm.md` marked this "possibly free, must be confirmed at a
registrar… treat this as unverified". It is not free:

```
longcut.app   registered 2026-04-26   expires 2027-04-26
              registrar: Cloudflare, Inc.
              nameservers: kay.ns.cloudflare.com, yisroel.ns.cloudflare.com
              status: client transfer prohibited
              A record: none
```

Registered five months ago, delegated to Cloudflare, serving nothing. The
registrant is redacted in RDAP, as it is for essentially every domain post-GDPR,
so **who holds it cannot be determined from public sources.** The earlier RDAP
attempt failed because `rdap.nic.google` is unreachable from this network; the
IANA bootstrap at `rdap.org` answers fine.

This is a domain fact, not a trademark fact. Registering a domain creates no
trademark rights and an unused parked domain is not use in commerce. It costs
Longcut the exact-match `.app`, nothing more — and `takethelongcut.com`, which is
the tagline anyway, is free.

## 8. What this screen cannot see

Listed so that nobody mistakes its silence for a finding.

- **Unregistered common-law users**, except the ones that happen to have a
  website or an App Store listing. §1 is the proof that this gap is real and
  large: the best-known user of "Scenic" in this exact field has no register
  presence whatsoever.
- **The 50 state trademark registers.** Not searched. State registration gives
  rights within that state.
- **Phonetic and foreign-equivalent variants**, beyond the edit-distance-1 and
  substring passes run here. A professional search covers sound-alikes
  systematically; this one covers spelling.
- **Design and composite marks** whose literal element differs but whose
  commercial impression does not.
- **Anything filed in the last few days** and not yet in the search index.
- **Whether a mark is strong enough to be worth registering** — a separate
  question from whether it is blocked, and the one `branding-brainstorm.md` §1
  answers for "Scenic".
- **Any opinion at all.** This document reports what is on public record. It does
  not advise on it.

## 10. Method, so it can be re-run

**System: the USPTO's public "Trademark search" at `tmsearch.uspto.gov`,
front-end version 2.0.1, searched 2026-09-19.** This is the system that
**replaced TESS**, which the USPTO retired at 12 p.m. on **2023-11-30**. Any
document telling you to search TESS is describing a system that no longer exists.

The basic word-mark searches are reproducible in the UI: pick **Wordmark** in the
dropdown, type the term, press the magnifier — note that the `?q=` URL parameter
is ignored and **Return does not submit**, you have to click the button.

The wildcard, fuzzy and class-restricted queries in this document were run
against the search system's own backend, which takes Elasticsearch `query_string`
syntax:

```
POST https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/prod-stage-v1-0-0/tmsearch
Content-Type: application/json

{"query":{"query_string":{"query":"wordmark:*longcut*"}},
 "size":50,"from":0,"track_total_hits":true,
 "_source":["wordmark","ownerName","id","registrationId",
            "internationalClass","alive","goodsAndServices",
            "filedDate","registrationDate","abandonDate","cancelDate",
            "currentBasis","disclaimer"]}
```

It is behind AWS WAF, so it must be called from a browser session that has
already loaded the site; `curl` alone will not reach it. Responses use
`hits.totalValue` and `hits.hits[].source` rather than the Elasticsearch
defaults. This is the application's private backend and the USPTO does not
document it as an API — it can change without notice, which is why the UI path is
given above as well.

**Fields used:** `wordmark`, `wordmarkPseudoText` (USPTO's own alternate-spelling
index), `internationalClass` (zero-padded, e.g. `009`), `alive`, `goodsAndServices`,
`ownerFullText`, `disclaimer`, `currentBasis`, `id` (serial number).

**Query shapes, per candidate:** exact token; every plausible respelling as an OR
set; `*substring*` across the whole register; `~1` fuzzy for one-character
variants; the two-word phrase; the same against the pseudo-mark field; and the
whole set again restricted to `internationalClass:(009 OR 039 OR 042)` and to
live marks. **Wildcard behaviour was verified before any zero was believed** —
`*scenic*` → 417, `*cut*` → 26,266 on the same field.

Common-law leg: `itunes.apple.com/search` for App Store names and seller details,
plain web search, RDAP via `rdap.org`, `whois`, and `dig` for delegation, plus
fetching each candidate domain to see what it actually serves.

**Sources that could not be retrieved from this machine**, stated rather than
substituted: `rdap.nic.google` (`.app` registry RDAP — the IANA bootstrap
answered instead), `whois.nic.co` and every `.co` RDAP endpoint tried, and
`detourist.com`.

## 11. What this changes

**Longcut stands, and Backway (§12) and Sideroad (§13) join it. Aimless, Detourist and Camber are
out, and `branding-brainstorm.md` §3 has no fourth candidate behind them** — the remaining "usable" rows there
(Dawdle, Vireo, Overlook) were never screened past the App Store count and would

Two knock-on points for `release-plan.md`, which is on
`claude/release-plan-sequence` and not yet on `main`:

- **§5 day-one item 2 is this document.** It asked for exactly this search, in
  twenty minutes, in classes 9 and 42. It took rather longer than twenty minutes,
  and **class 39 needs adding** — it is where half of Camber's blocking
  registration sits.
- **Decision 2's stated basis needs one qualification, not a reversal.** It
  declined paid clearance partly because the App Store check "returned zero
  collisions and zero in Navigation/Travel for the recommended candidate". That
  remains true of Longcut today, re-checked. But the same check rated Aimless
  clean the day before a Navigation-category competitor launched under that word,
  and it could never have seen Camber's registration or Detourist's site. The
  decision's own honest caveat — "no search of unregistered common-law rights, no
  coverage of close-but-not-identical marks, and no opinion" — is exactly the
  gap that produced three of the four results above.

Nothing here decides the name. That is Decision 2's twin in `release-plan.md` §8
and it belongs to the owner, after clearance and before the App Store Connect
record, because the bundle identifier becomes permanent at first submission.

(public relations) and `BACKBAY R/C` (electrical connectors). None is in travel

**This is a stronger register position than Longcut's**, which returned zero for
the token but had 49 two-word `LONG CUT` marks standing behind it.

### The real risk is the prefix, and it is worth naming

Searching the exact word finds nothing. Searching the *family* does:

| Mark | Owner | Reg. | Class | Goods |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`BACKTRACK`** | **Bushnell Inc.** | **4181999** | **009** | **"Global positioning receivers for use in navigation, hunting, and sports"** |
| `BACKROADS` | Backroads (California) | 2919546 | 039, 043 | Organizing and arranging tours |
| `BACKCOUNTRY` | Backcountry.com, LLC | 5522844 | 035, 039 | On-line retail; travel |

Bushnell's `BACKTRACK` is the one to put in front of an attorney: live, class 9,
and its goods are *GPS navigation devices*. Whether `BACKWAY` is confusingly
similar to `BACKTRACK` for navigation software is a real question — shared
prefixes alone are usually not enough, because the differing element carries the
distinction, but "usually" is doing work in that sentence and resolving it is
precisely what the paid opinion is for. This is the shape of risk Trap 2 exists
to catch: the exact-match search returns a clean zero and the similar-mark search
returns Bushnell.

### Common-law signals *(separate from the register above)*

- **`BackWay`** — Dares Core d.o.o., App Store, **Travel/Navigation**, released
  2012-05-26, v3.1.1, **2 ratings**. An offline "find your way back" waypoint and
  travel-log app. Company-profile databases record the Croatian startup behind it
  (Samobor, founded 2012) as inactive/deadpooled — **that is a secondary source
  and is labelled as one** — while the App Store listing remains live. Whether a
  still-listed app from a wound-up company is continuing use, or a mark abandoned
  through non-use, is an attorney question and a good one.
- **`Backway`** — Tarik El Hallaoui, App Store, Lifestyle/Travel, released
  2026-08-05, and `backway.ca`: *"Send shipments across Canada with Backway
  delivery app. Connect with verified drivers, get AI-powered pricing."* A
  shipment marketplace matching senders to drivers already making the trip.
  Different business, adjacent domain, six weeks old.
- **`backway.com`** — registered 1999-02-07 and currently serving an **active
  pornography site**. Stated because it is a material fact for a consumer brand,
  not because it is a trademark conflict: the goods are unrelated and it blocks
  nothing. It does mean the exact-match `.com` is unavailable and unattractive,
  and that the word carries an anatomical reading some users will reach first.
- **`thebackway.com`** — a personal journal, registered 2011.
- **"The Back Way"** is established terminology for the irregular migration route
  from The Gambia across the Sahara and Mediterranean to Europe, used in
  journalism and in peer-reviewed work. Not a legal issue. It is the kind of
  collision that surfaces in a review or a press mention rather than in a
  register.

### Domains

**`backway.app` is unregistered**, as is `backwayapp.com` — worth noting only
because `branding-brainstorm.md` §3 found every single-word `.app` it checked
already taken. `backway.com` and `thebackway.com` are both long since registered
and both in use, as above.

### Verdict

**No knockout blocker found — proceed to professional clearance.** The same
verdict as Longcut, reached on a cleaner register and a messier world.

Two questions for the attorney, in order: **`BACKTRACK`** (Bushnell, reg.
4181999, class 9, GPS navigation), and whether the 2012 `BackWay` app's mark
survives as use in commerce or has been abandoned.

Two facts that are not the attorney's call and belong to the owner: what sits on
`backway.com`, and the second meaning of the phrase. Neither blocks the name.
Both would be cheaper to think about now than after the bundle identifier is
permanent.

and a slightly worse version of it.
