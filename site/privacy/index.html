Sunday Drive Privacy Policy

Effective date: 4 October 2026

  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party code in the app.
  • Your location is used to follow your route. It is sent to our routing server to plan the route, and the server does not store it.
  • Two companies are in the path: Cloudflare carries the connection and can read it, and Oracle hosts the server.
  • The app does not record your drives.

What this app is

Sunday Drive plans and narrates driving routes that prefer scenic roads over fast ones. It runs on iPhone. It talks to one routing server, which the developer runs on a computer rented from Oracle Cloud in the United States. It uses Apple Maps for the map, for address search and for place names.

There is no account, no sign-in, and no way to create one.

What the app collects, and what it does not

No account and no identity. The app never asks for your name, email address, phone number or a password. The server has no user records to attach them to, and it neither asks for nor hands out any identifier.

No tracking, analytics or advertising. The app contains no third-party code at all: no analytics, no crash reporter, no ad network. It uses only Apple's own frameworks. It does not track you across apps, so it never asks for tracking permission.

No device identifiers. The app does not read your phone's advertising identifier, its vendor identifier or its name.

What is stored on your phone. The app keeps:

  • your five most recent destinations, each with its name and location, so the home screen can offer them again,
  • the length of the last loop you asked for,
  • the guidance voice you chose,
  • how long each voice takes to speak, so guidance can be timed,
  • whether voice guidance is muted,
  • whether you have seen the one-time "Before you drive" notice, and
  • whether the app follows your phone's light or dark appearance.

Location

The app uses your location for one thing: following the route you asked for. It asks for permission only while you are using the app, never "always". The message iOS shows you is:

Your location is used to follow the route, turn by turn, while you drive.

Location continues in the background during a drive, and the blue location indicator shows whenever it does. This is so a locked phone or an incoming call does not end your drive. Background location is switched off when navigation stops.

Location is precise. While you drive, the app asks iOS for the most precise location it offers, about once a second. That is what turn-by-turn guidance needs. If you have turned off Precise Location for the app, it asks to use precise location for the drive, and it cannot navigate without it.

Where your location goes

To two places, and no others.

1. To our routing server, to plan a route. When you ask for a route, the app sends your start and destination coordinates. When you ask for a loop, it sends your start point. If you leave the route while driving, it sends your current position and heading to plan a new one. Nothing goes with them: no identifier, no timestamp, no history of where you have been. The coordinates are sent inside the request itself, not in the web address.

The server stores nothing. It has no database and no log file, and it does not log requests. We checked the running server on 29 September 2026: after real route requests had passed through it, none of their coordinates appeared in any of its logs.

Oracle hosts the server. The server runs on a virtual machine that the developer rents from Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, in its US East region (Ashburn, Virginia). Your coordinates are held in that machine's memory while your route is worked out, and the server software writes them nowhere. Like any computer, the machine can temporarily move memory to disk when it runs short, and that could briefly include a request being worked on.

Oracle operates the hardware and the network, so a third party runs the machine that handles your coordinates. Oracle's own management software also runs on the machine: a component that reports processor and memory use to Oracle, a log collector that is set up to collect nothing, a security scanner, and a component for running remote commands. None of these is aimed at route requests.

Cloudflare carries the connection, and can read it. The app reaches the server through Cloudflare's network. Cloudflare decrypts the connection on its way through, which means it can technically read the whole request, coordinates included. Because the coordinates are not in the web address, they are not in the addresses that access logs record by default. What Cloudflare does with the traffic it carries is subject to Cloudflare's own logging and retention, not to this policy. It is the one point where your location passes through a third party on its way to the server, outside the developer's control.

2. To Apple, for the map, search and place names. The map is Apple's. When you type an address, it goes to Apple's search service. To name the place you are starting from, the app sends a coordinate to Apple. Apple handles that data under Apple's privacy policy, not this one. The addresses you type are never sent to our routing server, only the coordinates Apple turns them into.

Drive recordings

The app does not record your drives. While you drive, it uses your position to follow the route and keeps none of it: no log of where you went, when, or how fast. Nothing about a drive is saved when it ends.

Children

Sunday Drive is a driving app. It is not directed at children. It has no social features, nothing you create that other people can see, and no advertising.

Your choices

  • Refuse location. The app cannot plan or follow a route without it, but it reads nothing else about your phone.
  • Reset the stored destinations and settings by deleting the app.

There is no data about you on our server to request, correct or delete, because none is kept.
